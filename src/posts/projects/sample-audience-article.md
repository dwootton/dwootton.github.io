The best editorial experiences feel invisible: generous spacing, clear hierarchy, and just enough chrome to guide without shouting. This note summarizes a practical layout for essays that scales from phones to big screens while staying calm.

## Why a Rail TOC?

On desktop, a narrow left rail for a table of contents gives readers a quiet map without interrupting flow. It should be sticky, subtle, and single-level—no tree UI to wrestle with. On mobile, a simple disclosure keeps it out of the way until needed.

## Audience First, Always

Call out who the piece is for near the top. It sets expectation and makes it easy to bounce if you’re not the reader. Keep it short—one or two sentences beats a paragraph.

## Rhythm and Readability

Keep the body column around 700–760px on desktop with line-height near 1.7. Use hairline rules to separate meta and body, and allow occasional images to extend a bit past the text measure for breathing room. On mobile, let everything stack in a single column with comfortable side padding.
