I'm a PhD student at MIT CSAIL, advised by [Arvind Satyanarayan](https://arvindsatya.com/). My research designs intermediate representations for exploratory data analysis and interaction: formal languages that capture what analysts and interfaces are actually doing, so we can build better tools on top of them.

Recently this has meant CellQL, a SQL-derived IR that lets us compare what happens across notebook analyses; Meros, a grammar for composable direct-manipulation interaction; and AffordanceBench, an empirical study of how different kinds of tool documentation change LLM behavior.

I'm also thinking hard about embedded learning systems and how AI changes what apprenticeship can be. See [The Transfer Problem](https://computing.mit.edu/cross-cutting/social-and-ethical-responsibilities-of-computing/envisioning-the-future-of-computing-prize-home/), which won an honorable mention at MIT's Envisioning the Future of Computing Prize.

Before MIT I worked at Microsoft and Tableau Research. This summer I'll be at AI2 as a visiting HCI researcher.

Outside research, I'm a National Geographic Explorer for Arctic sea-ice visualization work, row with the MIT men's team, and have spent more time than is reasonable on small expeditions to cold places. I also volunteer with [Out for Undergrad](https://www.outforundergrad.org/), a nonprofit supporting LGBTQ+ undergraduates entering competitive industries.
